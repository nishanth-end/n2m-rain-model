How This Synthetic Dataset Was Created

To unblock full-stack engineering and model integration before the live satellite and gauge pipeline files arrive, a generator script (model/make_mock_dataset.py) simulates Bengaluru terrain and storm flooding with deterministic seed (seed = 1).

1. Spatial Grid: 600 synthetic cells spanning Bengaluru latitude (12.85° to 13.15°) and longitude (77.45° to 77.80°). Each cell receives realistic physical terrain distributions: elevation (DEM ~900m), slope, Topographic Wetness Index (TWI), HAND, impervious surface fraction (10% to 95%), and proximity to lakes and stormwater channels.

2. Storm Windows: 3 designated historical storm dates (2020-10-20, 2021-11-18, 2022-09-05) receiving heavy gamma-distributed rainfall (r1 ~ Gamma(2, 25)) plus 40 ordinary background days (r1 ~ Gamma(1, 6)).

3. Underlying Generative Logit: Flood outcomes are determined by physical interaction between rainfall, wetness, drainage height, and built-up fraction:

z = 0.05 * rain_3d_mm + 0.25 * twi - 0.35 * hand_m - 0.002 * dist_lake_m + 1.5 * imperv_frac - 5.5
P(flood_label = 1) = 1 / (1 + exp(-z))

Zero-Code Real Data Drop-in: When Member 1 provides the real flood_dataset.csv derived from CHIRPS precipitation, Copernicus DEM, and Sentinel-1 SAR imagery, placing it in data/flood_dataset.csv immediately switches the entire platform to real data without changing any application code.
