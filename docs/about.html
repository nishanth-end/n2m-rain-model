2. Problem Statement

Bengaluru has experienced repeated high-impact urban flooding events during monsoon storms (such as September 2022 and May 2022). Urban topography, rapid infrastructural concretization, culvert bottlenecks, and rajakaluve (stormwater canal) modifications make rainfall impact highly localized. While city-wide rain alerts exist, ward-level and neighborhood-level early warnings are critical to save property and protect citizens.

3. What the System Does

Rather than relying solely on rain gauges after water has already accumulated, the system provides anticipatory 24–48 hour flood hazard index scores. It accepts multi-day rainfall sequences, constructs rolling precipitation totals (1-day, 3-day, and 7-day), blends them with physical micro-terrain metrics (elevation, slope, Topographic Wetness Index, and imperviousness), and evaluates risk against an XGBoost model trained on historical flood events.

[FILL ME: Specific features of how warnings can be consumed by municipal ward officers, traffic police, or residents]

4. How It Works (Technical Overview)

Pipeline in Brief: Gridded precipitation time series + Digital Elevation Model (DEM) hydrological extraction → Event-based storm clustering → Sample-weighted XGBoost classifier → Dual temporal (Leave-One-Event-Out) and spatial (Ward-Grouped CV) validation → REST API & Interactive Map.

The system outputs a relative risk index mapped into four actionable hazard tiers: Low, Moderate, High, and Severe. Each prediction is accompanied by SHAP feature-level contributions detailing the exact physical factors driving the alert.
